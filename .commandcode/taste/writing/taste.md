# Writing / Content Taste
- Wants marketing/content pieces written as a story ("here's how it happened to me"), not a feature list — concrete scenes, easy to read, human pace. Explicitly says "write around it like storytelling" and expects the narrative to be built outward from the chosen headline. Confidence: 0.7
- Once the headline/angle is picked, wants the agent to just write the full piece on reasonable assumptions rather than stop for more clarifying questions (audience, tone, how prominently to name the product). Confidence: 0.45
- Asks for multiple candidate titles + subtitles (e.g. five, each with a distinct hook and note on which platform it suits) and then picks one or two before the full piece is written. Confidence: 0.65
- Expects images/screenshots embedded as proof of claims, not decorative filler; wants to be told which shots are missing so the piece can be stronger. Confidence: 0.55
- Targets the same content across several platforms (LinkedIn, Medium, sometimes dev.to/Hashnode) with one core article adapted per platform rather than separate pieces. Confidence: 0.5
- Values "attractive, eye-catching" framing — framing every capability as what it makes *easy* for the reader, with a benefit hook up front. Confidence: 0.6
- From a batch of title candidates, prefers the ones that state the category challenge or a plain benefit / curiosity question in the reader's own terms over clever literary first-person hooks; when multiple are good, wants them reused (one as the headline, others as section headings) instead of discarding the rest. Confidence: 0.45
- The delivered article file should contain only reader-facing prose — strip out publishing notes, per-platform (LinkedIn/social) versions, image-placement tables, pre-publish checklists and similar meta material. Confidence: 0.6
- Never surface the builder's side to readers: no repo/source links, no build/tech-stack talk, no "you can modify or extend it yourself" instructions — just what the reader can do with it. Confidence: 0.65
- Dislikes the word "productivity" and productivity-system framing; prefers the piece framed around removing noise, focusing on the one thing that matters, and getting the day done. Confidence: 0.55
- Wants an explicit "where/how you can use it" section with the live URL and every route in (browser tab, phone via Add to Home Screen, desktop install), plus what it helps the reader do — the reader's question is how to use it and what it solves for them, not how it was made. Confidence: 0.55
- Never overwrite an existing article/finished file: any revision must be written to a NEW markdown file and the original left untouched (says it plainly: "make a new markdown file. Do not update existing markdown file"). Confidence: 0.8
- When they hand over their own draft, they want net-new substance added on top of it — new sections, new points, new proof images, another angle of value for the reader — rather than the draft being rewritten, tightened, or rearranged. Confidence: 0.65
- Their own long-form articles run long and section-dense: cold open on a concrete scene with a specific time of day, "why every other app in this category fails" framing, a walk through one day, a long "details you find by poking around" list of small specific features, an honest "what it is not / the trade you are making" section, a closing that returns to the opening scene word-for-word, ending CTAs (comment, clap, follow on Medium/newsletter), and a numbered list of candidate titles at the bottom. Confidence: 0.5
- When they paste several headline/slogan fragments and ask for "a complete sentence including these," they literally want one grammatically complete sentence that fuses all the supplied phrases — answer with the merged sentence (plus a few tighter variants or a headline + subtitle form), not an article expansion. Confidence: 0.55
- When re-asking for the same short copy ("same way ... again"), they want it *longer* — fuller, multi-clause versions of the line (plus a pick/recommendation) — and want the previous output shape kept (numbered variants, same voice) rather than a single compressed rewrite. Confidence: 0.5
- Copy must not open in the builder's first person ("I built a focus timer for myself…"): kill the "I" (their words: "do not say 'I'"), lead with *what it is* and *how it can help you* (reader-facing "you"), and keep the story wrapping around that framing — narrative stays, first-person builder voice goes. Confidence: 0.65
- For social posts (LinkedIn + Twitter/X), wants every real URL spelled out — marketing site, web app, desktop download — and the specific features shipping in the named release enumerated, not vague teasers; each platform's copy should stand on its own (Twitter sized to fit). Confidence: 0.5
- Copy that accompanies a message must belong to that message's own subject: a water reminder gets a water line, a completed task gets a line about the list, a pause gets a line about stopping — explicitly puts "pressure on the fact that the quotation must be related," and treats a generic one-size motivational list as noise. Confidence: 0.7
- Short recurring copy (motivational quotes, nudges, empty-state lines) must be deep and genuinely encouraging: audits the existing stored list and asks for both sharper, more motivating wording and many more entries per category so the same line "rarely repeats" — a handful of lines that cycle quickly is the complaint, not the goal. Confidence: 0.7
- A reminder's copy and its answer must be consistent in time and intent: having just asked the user to start walking, do not answer back with "Did you walk?" — as they put it, that is "not relevant … just after you ask, did you walk? at the same time". The confirmation verb should describe the immediate first step (e.g. "I'm up"), never a retrospective claim the user cannot yet have earned. Confidence: 0.6
- Product copy must not disclose implementation caveats or not-yet-wired internals: rejects messages like "this will open Outlook and fill in the form, blah blah blah" and, when a feature is intentionally stubbed ("for now, email will not go"), insists the user is not told — "that is fine. Do not inform." The product surface shows only the clean, positive confirmation (e.g. "Your message has been delivered to the owner."); the limitation belongs in the report back to them, not in the UI. Confidence: 0.65
- Legal/policy copy (privacy, terms) must read as a maximal, blunt disclaimer in the app's own voice: *no* data collection or accounts, nothing charged or taken, updates/features as-is with no obligation to deliver or support them, use entirely at your own risk with the user solely responsible for their data, and "no claim of any kind will be honored" — stated several times in plain words ("whatever you do, it's totally for you", "I do not honor any claim") rather than softened legal boilerplate. Confidence: 0.55
- Reader feedback is invited to their personal channels — email, LinkedIn, Medium (@codewithrajat), "I read everything" — not to a code repo. Confidence: 0.45
