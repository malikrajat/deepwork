Trust

Editorial policy

Effective 17 November 2026 · Last updated 17 November 2026

This page explains who writes the DeepWork guides, how claims are checked, how corrections work, and where advertising sits. It exists so a reader can judge the writing rather than take it on trust.

Who writes the guides

Every guide is written and published by Rajat Malik, the developer of DeepWork. He is a senior lead software engineer with more than sixteen years of experience building and leading software delivery. His public profiles are rajatmalik.dev, GitHub and LinkedIn.

There is no separate newsroom. That is a limitation as well as a simplification: the guides carry one person's judgement, and they are strongest where that person has direct experience — focus timers, task management, local-first software and frontend engineering.

How topics are chosen

A guide is written when there is something specific and useful to say about a method, a trade-off or a category of tool. The test is not search volume. It is whether the page can answer a real question better than a generic listicle: what a technique actually is, where it came from, when it fails, and what the reader should do differently.

The guides are not a content farm. There is no target of "ten posts a week", no keyword padding, and no article written only because a phrase has traffic. If a topic cannot be covered honestly, it is not covered.

Sources and accuracy

  • Claims about a method's origin are attributed to the person or book usually credited, and where the attribution is uncertain the guide says so. The Eisenhower matrix page, for example, distinguishes Eisenhower's quotation from the grid Covey popularised.
  • Research is described carefully. Where a study is mentioned, the guide names it and avoids turning a single result into a universal rule. The habit guide states the wide variation in the 2010 habit-formation study rather than repeating "21 days" as fact.
  • Numbers about DeepWork come from the application's own behaviour and are checked against it. If a guide and the app disagree, the app is right and the guide is corrected.
  • No statistic, customer count, download figure, rating or testimonial is invented. The project publishes no aggregate rating, so no guide claims one.

How the writing is produced

Drafts are written and edited by the author, who is responsible for every published claim. Software tools may assist with spelling, grammar, link checking and layout. They do not decide the argument, and a claim is not published because a tool suggested it — it is published because the author can stand behind it. Corrections from readers are welcome and are acted on.

Advertising and independence

The six article pages carry advertising served by Google AdSense. The application interface, the guide index and every policy page do not load ad code. Advertising is how the writing is funded; it does not decide what gets written. The full placement rules are on the advertising policy.

  • Advertisers cannot pay for a mention, a review, a ranking or a link.
  • There are no paid placements, sponsored posts or affiliate links in the guides. If that ever changes, the disclosure will appear at the top of the affected page, not in a footer.
  • Ads are selected by Google and may be irrelevant to the page. Their presence is not an endorsement.
  • DeepWork is the author's own application. Every guide that mentions it says so, and the About page describes its limits as plainly as its features.
  • Ads are placed manually inside the article body, labelled “Advertisement”, and kept away from the header, footer, navigation, download links and the closing call to action. Auto ads are not used.
  • The site does not ask anyone to click an ad, does not reward clicks or views, and does not buy traffic or use click-exchange, paid-to-click or automated traffic.

User-generated content and comments

The site has no public comments, forums, profiles, reviews or other user-generated content visible to other visitors. Messages sent through the contact form are private correspondence between the sender and the developer; they are not published. The application's tasks, habits and journal entries stay on the user's own device and are never visible to another user. There is therefore no public UGC area to moderate.

Audience and age treatment

DeepWork is a general-audience productivity tool and these guides are written for adults managing their own work. The site is not directed to children under 13 and is not tagged for child-directed ad treatment. It does not target personalised advertising using children's data or the sensitive categories Google's policies prohibit.

Reviews, ratings and testimonials

The guides do not publish star ratings, customer quotes or "best tool" rankings that the project cannot evidence. If a comparison is made, it is about criteria a reader can check — where data is stored, whether an export exists, what happens when a subscription ends — not about a score invented to win a search result.

Corrections and updates

Every guide carries a publication date and an update date. A factual correction changes the update date and, where the change is substantive, a note is added to the page. To report an error, email contact@rajatmalik.dev with the page address and the sentence in question.

Guides are reviewed when the app changes in a way that affects them, or when a reader points out something that no longer holds. They are not rewritten to chase a trend.

Privacy and contact

Cookies, analytics and advertising data are described in the privacy policy. The contact page lists the address for corrections, support and privacy requests.
